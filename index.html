Discovery

Spinal Cord Injury

Patrick DECHERCHI, born in 1970 in Marseille, is a French professor of neuroscience working at the Institute of Movement Sciences, a joint research unit of Aix-Marseille University and the CNRS. He holds a Ph.D. in neurobiology/neurophysiology and is certified to supervise doctoral research. After defending his doctoral thesis, he joined the DGA, which seconded him to the AP-HM as a military research engineer for one year, then moved to London to join the neurobiology laboratory at the National Institute for Medical Research, led by Professor Geoffrey RAISMAN, considered by the scientific community to be the father of neural plasticity.

Patrick DECHERCHI has been instrumental in making significant scientific advances in the field of spinal cord injury. In 2002, he received the Jansen Prize from the French National Academy of Medicine for his work on spinal cord repair using cells derived from the olfactory bulb and peripheral nerves.

Unlike other scientists working on spinal cord repair who focus solely on motor pathways, Patrick DECHERCHI believes that patients with spinal cord injuries will only be able to walk again if the nervous tissue is fully reconstructed. In his view, motor function can only be fully restored if both motor and sensory pathways are repaired.

He serves as an expert for the European Union, the Ministry of National Education, and the Ministry of Higher Education, Research, and Space, and is the author of numerous scientific publications.

He is also reportedly a member of a very influential international organization.
